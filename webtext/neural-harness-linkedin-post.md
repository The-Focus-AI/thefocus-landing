Overnight jobs parsing financial deal structures, monitoring logs, responding to events. The first plan tapped out, I added another, that one tapped out too.

Here's the thing: this isn't weird. It's a pattern we've seen before.

Computing accretes native units by composing the layer below. Adders make CPUs. CPUs make machines. Boolean logic makes operating systems. Lexers and parsers make compilers. Programs and networks make services. Services and databases make applications. Each layer is just the layer below, composed.

Everything on that list sits on top of Boolean logic. Silicon, gates, if/then — all deterministic, all ones and zeros.

Neural nets aren't more of that. They're a different kind of logic. Pattern, association, fuzzy matching, generation. We added a new floor and a new kind of computation that doesn't reduce to "if A and B then C."

By themselves these things predict tokens. They don't loop, don't read files, don't remember. To get work out of one you wrap it — a loop, tools, file access, a shell, context management. That wrapper is the harness.

The neural harness is to neural nets what the compiler was to source code.

It's stopped being a tool I reach for. It's just the tool. Coding, PDFs, invoices, deal docs, family infographics, health tracking — same unit, different work.

"Coding agent" is vestigial. The thing isn't a coding agent. It's a harness around a model, and what runs in it is whatever you have tools for.

And the model debate? It's pitched one floor below where the action is. It's not model-vs-model. It's harness-with-default-model vs other-harness-with-default-model. Terminal-Bench 2 showed 25 points of accuracy difference on the same Opus 4.6 — just from picking a different harness.

The harness IS a model strategy. Picking a harness means picking which models do which jobs inside it.
